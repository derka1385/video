# STILL HERE

*A short film. 2 min 42 s.*

Five friends at a dinner table at night. Candles, a window full of city lights.
Everyone is speaking English, until, without anyone deciding to, three of them
aren't.

The film never shows the language on screen and never subtitles it. The
audience hears what Sam hears. The Portuguese dialogue and its translation are
below for anyone making or studying the film. The joke is that it's kind: while
they leave Sam out, they are planning to bring her along.

---

### CAST

| | | voice |
|---|---|---|
| **SAM** | our protagonist; speaks English only | Kokoro `bf_emma` |
| **LU** | tells the story; Brazilian | Kokoro `pf_dora` |
| **RAFA** | the story is about him; Brazilian | Kokoro `pm_alex` |
| **BIA** | Brazilian, grew up abroad | Kokoro `af_bella` |
| **NOOR** | new to the group; quiet all evening; never speaks | — |

### 1. BELONGING  *(0:00 – 0:26)*

Black. Room tone rises: a flat at night, traffic far below, a glass touched.
LU's voice arrives before the picture does.

> **LU** No, wait, wait. It gets worse. So he walks in, sits down, and asks for the menu.
> **RAFA** Don't.
> **LU** And the woman just looks at him, and goes: Sir. This is a flower shop.

Laughter all around the table. Fade up on the table from its far end: two
candles, glasses, five people, the window full of bokeh. NOOR sits at the far
end, backlit, easy to miss.

> **RAFA** It looked like a restaurant!
> **SAM** In his defence, it really does look like a restaurant.
> **RAFA** Thank you! Sam gets me.
> **BIA** Okay, but then you bought the tulips.

The biggest laugh of the night. SAM laughs with them. She is in the middle of it.

> **RAFA** I panicked! What was I supposed to do?

### 2. THE SWITCH  *(0:26 – 0:52)*

Still laughing, LU says something to RAFA, and it isn't English. Nobody
notices. The camera settles on SAM's face in one long take and stays.

> **LU** *Não, mas sério. Você viu a cara dela?*  — No, but seriously. Did you see her face?
> **RAFA** *Vi, pô. Eu queria sumir.*  — I did, man. I wanted to disappear.
> **BIA** *E as* **tulipas**? *Você deu pra quem, no fim?*  — And the tulips? Who did you give them to, in the end?

The one word she knows lands with unnatural clarity. Beside her glass, on the
polished wood, words surface: *I know that word.*

> **RAFA** *Dei pra minha mãe. Ela amou.*  — I gave them to my mom. She loved them.

They laugh. SAM laughs along, a small breath through the nose. Above her glass:
*I think I understood that.*

> **LU** *Falando nisso, vocês vão no aniversário da* **Carol**, *sábado?*  — Speaking of which, are you all going to Carol's birthday on Saturday?
> **BIA** *Vou, mas vou chegar tarde. Tô de plantão até as nove.*  — Yes, but I'll be late. I'm on shift until nine.
> **RAFA** *A casa dela é longe pra caramba, né?*  — Her place is really far, isn't it?

SAM'S POV, wide lens: the three of them facing each other, a closed circle. In
the dark at the edge of the frame: *Wait.*

> **LU** *A gente vai junto. Eu passo aí e pego vocês.*  — We'll go together. I'll come by and pick you up.

*Maybe…*

The voices have started to lose their edges. Consonants go first.

### 3. "SAM"  *(0:52 – 1:04)*

> **LU** *(turning to look at Sam)* *Ah, a gente devia levar a* **Sam** *também. Ela ia adorar.*  — Oh, we should bring Sam too. She'd love it.
> **RAFA** *(looking at her, smiling)* *Ia mesmo. Mas lá é tudo em português, coitada.*  — She would. But it's all in Portuguese there, poor thing.

A laugh, strangely sharp, with a long tail. SAM smiles automatically. A heartbeat
enters, barely.

> **BIA** *Ah, ela se vira. A gente traduz.*  — Oh, she'll manage. We'll translate.

Close on SAM, long lens. The room behind her has started to go. Near her face:
*Were they talking about me?* The words crumble upward.

### 4. INWARD  *(1:04 – 1:44)*

From behind her shoulder the camera moves in while the lens widens: SAM stays
the same size, and everything she is looking at falls away. The others soften
into warm light and drift to the horizon. The walls come apart from the edges of
her vision inward. The table spreads out into a black mirror with no edge. The
candle flame lifts, grows, and hangs in front of her: a small amorphous light.
This is **interpretation**.

The conversation goes on, but now as distance: low-passed, stretched, far
reverb, slowly turning into a choir made of their voices.

> *A Carol vai fazer aquela feijoada de novo?*  — Is Carol making that feijoada again?
> *Tomara. Foi a melhor coisa que eu comi na vida.*  — I hope so. Best thing I've ever eaten.

The light reaches for a meaning. Particles gather into a **tulip**: maybe
they're still on the story. A laugh. The tulip comes apart.

> *Você fala isso de toda comida.*  — You say that about every meal.

*Should I say something?* hangs under the light and fades.

The light tries again: **three figures leaning together, one a little apart.**
It breaks. Then **a lit doorway: three inside, one outside.** On the mirror floor:
*They probably didn't notice.*

> *E o presente? Vamos fazer uma vaquinha?*  — And the present? Shall we all chip in?
> *Uma planta! Ela adora planta.*  — A plant! She loves plants.
> *Tulipa não, por favor.*  — No tulips, please.

The biggest laugh of the night again, far away and too bright. The light makes
**her own profile**, and it shatters.

Fragments repeat on their own: *a Sam… coitada…* and laughter that echoes
without anyone laughing.

### 5. DARK  *(1:44 – 1:58)*

The camera pulls away until SAM is a small shape at the edge of a mirror in a
very large dark. The others are four lights on the horizon, flaring faintly when
they laugh. The light in front of her shrinks to an ember. The score thins to a
low drone and stops. Only her breathing and her heart are left.

In the dark, lit only by the ember: *I'm still here.*

Silence.

### 6. RETURN  *(1:58 – 2:23)*

From very far away:

> **LU** Oh. Sorry.

The ember answers the voice.

> **LU** We switched languages.

Sound returns first: high frequencies, then clarity, then the room tone. A warm
major chord blooms under it. She lets out a breath she didn't know she was
holding.

> **RAFA** Sorry, we didn't even realise.

The light comes back outward from the ember and rebuilds the room around it.
The ember is a candle flame again. Faces, depth, the window.

> **BIA** Sam, you're coming on Saturday, right?

She doesn't know what Saturday is.

> **SAM** Yeah.
> **LU** Yes! Okay. But nobody is bringing tulips.
> **RAFA** It was one time!

Everyone laughs, in English. It's warm again. Only a few seconds of real time
have passed.

### 7. NOOR  *(2:23 – 2:42)*

> **LU** Honestly, though? It was a really nice flower shop.
> **BIA** It was! The lady was so sweet about it.

The camera stays on SAM while the table carries on. She looks at them. Then she
looks down the length of the table.

Over her shoulder, focus racks to NOOR at the far end, who has hardly spoken
all night. They're turning a glass. A small point of candlelight shows in it.
NOOR looks up.

SAM takes a breath to say something to them —

Cut to black. Silence.

> *Sometimes exclusion isn't intentional.*
>
> *But it is still felt.*

**STILL HERE**

### Sound, in one paragraph

Sound is the film's main device. Everything Sam hears from the others passes
through a time-varying "hearing" filter driven by one curve, *clarity*. As it
falls, voices lose their consonants (a steep low-pass), gain a muffled low-mid
body, spread wider and hand themselves over to a dark 7.5-second reverb. Words
she recognises (*tulipas*, *Carol*, *Sam*) and a few random syllables snap back
unnaturally clear. Laughter keeps a narrow presence band, so it stays sharp
after the words have gone. Her own voice, breath and heartbeat are never
filtered. The score is synthesised: additive drones in D minor, bowed-glass
tones for each interpretation, and the Portuguese dialogue itself paulstretched
and tuned into a choir. At the return, the room tone is the most comforting
sound in the film.
